01 / A living tradition

Every fire carries
a story.

“We carry the friendship of yesterday into the campfire of today.”
The spirit of our ash ceremony

The campfire ash ceremony, also known as a friendship ash ceremony, is a tradition shared by Scouts and Guides. A small amount of ash from an earlier campfire is added to a new one, linking gatherings across places, generations and friendships.

Scouting tradition traces the custom to the first Scout camp on Brownsea Island in 1907. The story tells that Lord Baden-Powell, the founder of Scouting, kept some ashes and shared them with the Scouts who attended. Ashes were then carried to later campfires, with a little collected again to continue the journey.

The provenance of every entry in an ash list can be difficult to establish. But the heart of the ceremony is the spirit behind it: imagining the songs, laughter, adventures and friendships of all those campfires, kept alive as we gather around our own.

Our register is a record of the history passed on with these ashes. It honours the tradition and the memories attached to it; it is not an independently verified chain of provenance.

02 / Friendship without borders

From the campfire
to the airwaves.

International Scouting and Guiding help young people discover that they belong to a wider world. Meeting people with different customs and cultures builds understanding, friendship and the foundations of peace.

Not everyone can travel to an international camp. JOTA-JOTI brings that opportunity closer to home, connecting Scouts and Guides through amateur radio and the internet. Like our shared ashes, every conversation adds another friendship to the story.

04 / Keep the friendship glowing

Pass the spirit on.

A simple suggested ceremony for your next campfire.

“Into this fire we bring the memories of campfires before us. May their friendship warm our gathering, and may the ashes we carry onwards remind us that Scouts and Guides everywhere are part of one brother and sisterhood of peace.”

Suggested words for your gathering.

  1. Share the story

    Introduce the tradition and read a few places from the ash register. Invite everyone to imagine the friendships made there.

  2. Bring the memories together

    A responsible adult adds a small amount of the inherited ashes to the campfire, following the venue’s fire rules.

  3. Carry the friendship onwards

    After the fire is fully extinguished and the ashes are completely cold, a responsible adult collects a small amount in a labelled container. Keep the register with them and add the name, place and year of your gathering.
